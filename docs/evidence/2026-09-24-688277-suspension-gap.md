## Contract reading

The rejection is not a defect in this service:

- **BR-022** requires one request to be atomic over the rows the source *declares*, and says
  the adapter "never sorts, deduplicates, fills or mixes fields". It names `resumption`
  among the legitimate moves that must be preserved rather than rejected, and assigns jump
  confirmation to the consumer's own evidence boundary: "provider admission is not
  confirmation."
- **BR-023** states that the current-session gate "does not infer exchange holidays".
- **BR-036** binds pagination exactness per page; it is a cardinality contract, not a
  claim that the exchange calendar has no holes.

So `complete=true` on this instrument asserts that every declared row of every requested
page decoded and validated -- not that no trading day was suspended. A traded-day
continuity check is a separate policy that has to be enforced, with its own evidence, by
whoever owns it.

## What this artifact does not settle

BR-092 lives in the downstream's own gateway/old local service, not in this repository, so
this artifact cannot change it. It supplies the missing evidence for that decision: the
hole is a real suspension, and an unconditional traded-day continuity rule will keep
rejecting it. A verifiable exception needs exchange-announcement evidence; simply relaxing
the check, or letting a provider guess holidays, would trade a false rejection for silent
data corruption.

## Re-running

1. Build a request body for `HistoricalBars` with `limit` 800 and no `start`/`end`, and
   encode it as the base64 `payload.data` (the schema is `magic.market.historical_bars.request`).
2. Send it with `grpcurl` using the client bundle's mTLS material and
   `-authority magic-market.local`; `preferred_provider` pins the source.
3. Inspect the returned rows: `bar_start` is the exact source date, so the sequence can be
   compared against the exchange calendar directly. `target\runtime\probe\` holds the local
   scratch wrappers used here (`bars-dates.ps1`, `call-hist-plain.cmd`); they are not part of
   the deliverable.
