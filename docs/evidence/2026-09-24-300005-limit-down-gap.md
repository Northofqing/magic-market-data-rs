# 300005's two limit-down closes, 2026-07-17 and 2026-07-20 -- 2026-09-24

The downstream's local route rejected `300005`'s 90-day batch at its own BR-171 economic-jump
gate, quoting a close-to-close move of `18.59 -> 14.87 = -20.0108%` on 2026-07-17 and asking
for manual confirmation. This artifact records what the rows actually are: two consecutive
limit-down closes, so the sequence is a market fact and the gate is firing on a legitimate
move rather than a defect.

It is the second of the two residuals the 2026-09-23 Tdx work left open -- the first being
the `688277` suspension gap in
[2026-09-24-688277-suspension-gap.md](2026-09-24-688277-suspension-gap.md) -- and it is
re-runnable in the same way.

The two flagged days share the shape that identifies a limit-down on a ChiNext equity:

- the close **equals the day's low** on both days, and
- the move is the exchange's **20 percent limit**: `18.59 x 0.8 = 14.872 -> 14.87` and
  `14.87 x 0.8 = 11.896 -> 11.90`.

Volume on the two days is 2.7x and 3.2x the 2026-07-16 row, which is what a limit-down on
heavy volume looks like. The sequence then opens the next session lower and recovers
intraday on 2026-07-21, so the run of limit-downs ends there.

## Cross-source agreement

`Sina` returns the identical readings for `2026-07-16 .. 2026-07-21` (closes `18.59`,
`14.87`, `11.90`, `11.04`; lows equal to the closes on 07-17 and 07-20; matching volumes).
So two independent sources carry the same two limit-down rows; neither is a decoding or
pagination artifact of a single provider.

## Contract reading

This is not provider-integrity territory:

- **BR-022** requires one request to be atomic over the rows the source declares, forbids the
  adapter to sort, deduplicate, fill or mix, and states explicitly that a fixed adjacent-close
  percentage "is not a provider-integrity rule" -- legitimate IPO, relisting, resumption,
  corporate-action and market-price moves may exceed it and must be preserved. It assigns the
  policy to the consumer: "provider admission is not confirmation."
- **BR-036** bounds page cardinality and ordering, not the size of an economic move.

So a jump gate firing here is a policy decision made on the consumer's side, and it is a
reasonable one to *keep* -- the artifact's point is that its evidence must be the exchange
fact, not the provider's admission state. A gate relaxed to silence this case would equally
silence a genuinely corrupt row.

## What this artifact does not settle

BR-171 lives in the downstream's own gateway, not in this repository, so no change here
follows from these readings. What the readings supply is the confirmation basis the gate
asks for: both flagged days close at their low with a move equal to the limit, on
above-average volume, on two independent sources. Whether to automate that check (for
example, "close equals low and the move equals the exchange limit") or to bind each case to
an announcement is the rule owner's decision.

## Re-running

The method is identical to the `688277` capture: build a `HistoricalBars` request with
`limit` 800 and no `start`/`end`, encode it as the base64 `payload.data`
(`magic.market.historical_bars.request`), send it with `grpcurl` using the client bundle's
mTLS material and `-authority magic-market.local`, then decode each record's base64 `data`
and read `bar_start` / `close` / `volume`. Local scratch wrappers live under
`target\runtime\probe\` (`call-hist-plain.cmd`, `bars-dates.ps1`) and are not deliverables.
